01 · Problem

The gap nobody filled

Competitive audit → 12 apps mapped
12 apps audited
0 covered both

A competitive audit of 12 solo travel apps found a consistent split: apps either offered basic SOS (no offline fallback) or itinerary planning (no safety features). The intersection, real-time alerts plus offline capability, was absent from the market.

Primary research confirmed what the data suggested: solo women travellers made safety compromises constantly, avoiding neighbourhoods, manually checking in with friends, skipping activities, because the tools available left them unsupported.

02 · Research

What the numbers told us

Field notes → raw quotes from solo travellers
"I always text a friend before I go out alone."
"I avoid this street after dark. Just a feeling."
"I'd trust a verified traveller more than a stranger."
"Raw crime alerts freak me out more than they help."

↓ synthesized into 4 key insights

👩

59% of solo travellers are women

Yet existing apps were gender-neutral at best. No app centred women's specific safety needs.

📡

Offline is a survival feature

Many high-risk scenarios (remote areas, crowded events, foreign SIM issues) occur exactly when connectivity is lowest.

🤝

Trust in strangers is high

90% of solo travellers said they'd met others and felt safer travelling together. Verification was the missing trust layer.

📍

Alerts needed context, not raw data

Raw crime data is anxiety-inducing. Users needed relative risk: "safer than average" vs "avoid after 9pm."

05 · Research

Research outcomes

Research paper (2024): The underlying research on solo travel safety gaps was written up as a formal paper, validating both the identified market gap and the proposed solution framework.

Key takeaway incorporated: separating "danger" (binary, high-alert) from "awareness" (contextual, ambient), and gave ambient awareness without triggering anxiety spirals.
