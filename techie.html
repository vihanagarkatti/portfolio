Techie: Retail Store Chatbot

A QR-triggered conversational assistant for electronics retail that cuts through spec overload. No app download needed: scan, ask, decide. Published in Springer, presented at Cambridge University.

68% of electronics shoppers leave without buying due to information overload
QR triggered, zero app download friction, instant product context
3 intent modes: compare, recommend, explain, matched to user goal
2024 Springer publication + Cambridge conference presentation

QR scan entry: zero download

Chat: plain language answers

Spec explainer: 120Hz simplified

Staff handoff: always available

01 · Problem

Information overload kills sales and wastes time

Comparison: Galaxy S25 vs iPhone

"What is 120Hz?" answered simply

Electronics retail is a paradox of choice problem. A single shelf can hold 8 TVs with 15+ specs each. Staff are stretched during peak hours. Result: 68% of shoppers report leaving without buying because they couldn't make a confident decision.

Existing solutions dump information without helping process it. The opportunity: a conversational layer that translates raw specs into decisions. Contextual, instant, zero download friction.

02 · Research

Three weeks of in-store observation

⏱️

Avg. 23 mins of browsing

Most of that time spent re-reading the same spec cards, not exploring new options. Decision paralysis, not discovery.

🙋

Only 40% asked staff

Felt embarrassed, couldn't find staff, or didn't want pressure. A chatbot removes all three barriers simultaneously.

🔁

"Compare" was the #1 ask

"What's the difference between X and Y" was the most common staff question: a perfect conversational use case.

📱

QR > App store

Shoppers refused to download a retailer app. QR on the shelf, no install, no account, had near-zero friction in testing.

04 · Publication

Academic validation

Springer (2024): Research on conversational UX in physical retail accepted and published in Springer conference proceedings. Validates the problem framing and QR-as-entry-point as the novel contribution.

Cambridge University: Presented findings to a research audience. Key feedback: the QR-triggered entry eliminated download friction as the primary adoption barrier, confirmed as the most novel design decision.
