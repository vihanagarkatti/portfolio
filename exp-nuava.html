About the project

Nuava set out to digitise school sports management — a space dominated by spreadsheets, WhatsApp groups, and physical notice boards. The product needed to serve coaches who were experts in sport, not software — usability was the central design constraint from day one.

Contribution 01

Competitive Analysis & Feature Prioritisation

Before designing anything, I researched how established sports management platforms approached the same problem — looking for patterns worth adopting and gaps worth filling.

Benchmarking PlayHQ and SportEasy

Conducted competitor analysis of established sports management platforms to understand standard feature sets, navigation patterns, and friction points. Research directly informed which features to prioritise for Nuava's initial release — focusing on fixture scheduling and tournament tracking as the highest-value workflows.

Avoiding feature traps

The competitive audit revealed which features competitors had built that users found unnecessarily complex. Nuava's first release scope was deliberately narrower — better to do three things excellently than ten things adequately.

Impact

Competitor research shaped the first-release scope, helping the team avoid rebuilding feature sets users already found complex elsewhere — a direct contribution to product strategy, not just design execution.

Contribution 02

Core Product Design — Dashboard & Three Modules

Designed the foundational structure of the coach-facing web app — from the dashboard that would serve as daily home base to the three core feature modules.

Information-dense dashboard

Designed the core dashboard and navigation patterns — balancing information density (upcoming fixtures, recent results, pending tasks all at a glance) against first-time usability for non-technical coaches who were used to spreadsheets.

Fixture scheduling flows

Designed for the common case (quick recurring scheduling) without sacrificing flexibility for edge cases (rescheduling, weather cancellations, multi-team conflicts). Replaced a WhatsApp-and-spreadsheet process with a structured digital flow.

Tournament bracket management

Designed the interface for elimination brackets and round-robins — translating visually complex structures into something coaches could set up and update without external tools. Iterated on hierarchy and visual encoding of match results.

Student performance tracking

Designed views for tracking individual student development across seasons — useful for team selection decisions and parent communications. Focused on trend-at-a-glance rather than raw data tables.

Impact

Contribution 03

Engineering Collaboration

Worked directly with the engineering team throughout the design process — an early lesson in designing with implementation constraints in mind rather than handing off finished designs cold.

Feasibility-aware design process

Surfaced feasibility questions early by involving engineers in design reviews rather than handing off at the end. Adjusted designs where needed to fit sprint scope without compromising core usability goals — learning when to push back and when to adapt.

What this taught me

Designing in close collaboration with engineering produces faster, more realistic outcomes. It also taught me to defend design decisions with user reasoning rather than preference — the only argument that holds in a sprint review.
