Service Design Study: Azorte

An end-to-end service design analysis of Azorte, Bengaluru's neo-fashion retail store, exploring how technology-enabled nudges and physical touchpoints influence purchase decisions, and redesigning the touchpoints that weren't working.

5touchpoint stages mapped across the customer journey
3weeks of in-store observational research in Bengaluru
12+friction points identified, prioritised by impact × frequency
4redesigned touchpoints with service blueprints

Customer emotion journey across 5 stages: the decision stage showed the steepest drop-off, driving the redesign focus

01 · Context

Neo-fashion retail, reimagined with tech

Azorte is a Reliance-backed neo-fashion retail concept, a hybrid between traditional fashion retail and a tech-enabled experience store. It uses smart mirrors, digital screens, and QR-linked product info to create a modern in-store experience.

The research question: are these digital touchpoints actually influencing purchase decisions, or are they visual noise? The study set out to map the full customer journey and identify where the experience created value vs. friction.

02 · Research Methods

Three weeks of observational fieldwork

👀

Shadowing (30+ journeys)

Observed customers without interaction. Tracked dwell time at each touchpoint, body language, and moments of hesitation.

🗺️

Experience mapping

Mapped physical movement patterns. Identified "dead zones" where customers rarely went and high-traffic convergence areas.

💬

Exit interviews

Brief 3-question interviews with customers who browsed but didn't buy. Consistent finding: "couldn't decide" and "couldn't find help."

📐

Service blueprinting

Mapped frontstage (customer actions) against backstage (staff, systems) to find misalignments and opportunity areas.

03 · Key Findings

The tech was there to impress, not assist

"The digital screens were everywhere, but they were playing brand videos, not helping customers make decisions. High-gloss content, zero utility."

📺

Screens for brand, not customer

Digital displays showed lookbooks and brand videos. Customers ignored them after 5 seconds. No product information, no comparison tools.

🪞

Smart mirrors underused

Smart mirrors offered virtual try-on but were buried at the back, so only customers who already knew about them sought them out.

🏷️

Price tags triggered hesitation

Customers picked items up, checked prices, and put them down, with no supporting info on quality, material, or alternatives at hand.

🚶

Staff at wrong points

Staff concentrated near the entrance (greeter role) but absent in the middle of the store where decision-making actually happened.

04 · Redesign

Four touchpoint redesigns

Nudge 01

Decision-support screens at browse zone

Nudge 02

Nudge 03

Nudge 04

05 · Reflection

What service design taught me

Technology is not a strategy. Azorte had more tech investment than most retail spaces in Bengaluru, but deployed without a clear customer job-to-be-done. Beautiful screens doing nothing useful are expensive wallpaper.

Service design is systems thinking. You can't fix a digital touchpoint in isolation: staff positioning, physical flow, digital layer, and business goals all have to be considered together. This shaped how I think about any product system.
