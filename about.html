🍶 Hello there

I'm Viha Nagarkatti

I'm a product designer and HCI Master's student at the University of Maryland. I think in systems, build in Figma, and have a genuine soft spot for hand-drawn things (as you may have noticed).

I'm drawn to design problems that sit at the intersection of human behaviour and social impact, the kind where getting the details right actually matters. My work spans mobile apps, conversational UI, service design, and publication design, always grounded in real research before anything gets near a canvas.

My research has been published in Springer and presented at Cambridge University, which still surprises me when I say it out loud. It's reinforced a conviction I hold strongly: design without evidence is decoration.

How I think about design

On research

I never design for assumptions. The most important design work happens before the canvas opens: in interviews, in observations, in understanding why people do what they do, not what they say they do.

On simplicity

Clarity is the hardest thing to design. Anyone can add features. The discipline is knowing what to cut, what serves the user's actual goal versus what serves the product's internal complexity.

On systems

Good design is invisible. When the system is well-built, users don't notice the design; they just accomplish what they came to do. That invisibility is the goal, not a failure.

On impact

I'm drawn to problems that matter. Not every project needs to change the world. But when a design decision could meaningfully improve someone's safety, access, or daily life, that's where I bring my full attention.

On craft

The details are the design. Pixel-perfect spacing isn't vanity; it communicates care. Users may not consciously notice the 8px instead of 12px, but they feel the difference.
